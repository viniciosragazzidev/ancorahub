# Isometric voxel style specification

## Scope

This specification is calibrated from accepted icons generated by this skill. The bundled assets are self-contained internal outputs, not user-provided or third-party reference images. Use them to transfer construction, camera, material, density, lighting, and color-allocation behavior only.

## Construction grammar

- Establish one coarse cubic base module for the whole series.
- Build the subject from a small number of large rectilinear voxel blocks. Every block dimension must be an integer multiple of the base module.
- Merge adjacent occupied cells into 2×1×1, 2×2×1, or larger cuboids and hide internal coplanar seams. Large planes should read as single blocks, not tiled cube grids.
- Keep block faces planar and edges crisp. Avoid bevel highlights and rounded edge catches.
- Build curves as a few intentional steps. Never hide a smooth polygonal mesh beneath a voxel-like surface texture.
- Use a standard dominant span of 7–12 base modules, with a hard range of 6–14.
- Target about 12–30 visible blocks per subject; treat 40 as a hard ceiling unless the user explicitly approves more.
- Keep thin parts at least one base module thick. Never introduce a half-scale detail grid.
- Allow sparse intentional gaps when they clarify eyes, mouths, handles, or negative space.

## Series voxel scale

- Lock the projected base-module edge before producing a series.
- Target one projected base-module edge at 5–7% of the canvas short side. For a 1254×1254 output, use approximately 63–88 px.
- Keep projected base-module size within ±10% across accepted assets in the series.
- Keep camera zoom, projection, and base-module proportions constant. Visible blocks may be larger integer-multiple cuboids.
- Fit complex subjects by merging masses, hiding coplanar seams, reducing secondary detail, and keeping only two or three recognition cues. Never solve fit with a finer grid.

## Camera and projection

- Use an orthographic three-quarter isometric view.
- Elevate the camera enough to show top faces while keeping the front silhouette dominant.
- Show the front, one side, and top along coherent axes across the entire object.
- Avoid perspective convergence, wide-angle distortion, top-down views, and eye-level side views.
- Rotate the object slightly when needed for recognition, but preserve the same projection logic.

## Composition

- Present one isolated focal object or one tightly coupled pair.
- Center the subject or offset it only slightly.
- Let the object occupy roughly 55–72% of a square frame.
- Preserve generous neutral-light negative space on every side.
- Keep the object visibly suspended. Preserve uninterrupted empty background beneath it.
- Cast no contact shadow or readable grounding shadow. A barely perceptible, broad, diffuse cast haze is acceptable only when it does not imply a supporting surface.
- Do not add a horizon, platform, room, landscape, or decorative scene.

## Backdrop consistency

- Generate, evaluate, and deliver every asset on one flat `#F4F4F4` field. Do not allow each generation to choose a nearby gray.
- Treat the raw model background as intermediate even when it looks correct. After the object passes the visual gate, normalize only border-connected near-gray pixels to exact `#F4F4F4`.
- Never rerender an accepted object solely to correct the background. Normalization must not change silhouette, voxel occupancy, colors, lighting, scale, framing, edge quality, or canvas dimensions.
- Deliver an opaque RGB PNG. Do not add an alpha channel or transparent canvas.
- Keep the generated border flat enough for deterministic normalization: no vignette, gradient, texture, haze, colored spill, or object touching a canvas edge.
- Validate clean corner and below-object patches. They must all measure exact RGB `(244, 244, 244)` in the final.

## Form and recognition

- Start from a readable silhouette.
- Preserve only two or three iconic cues: for example rays plus circular core for a sun, crescent cutout plus tips for a moon, or bars plus upward arrow for a stock market.
- Exaggerate important cues enough to survive voxel simplification.
- Prefer chunky proportions, friendly imbalance, and compact massing.
- Leave surfaces plain unless an internal mark is necessary for recognition. Do not add decorative squares that could read as eyes or faces.
- Avoid fragile protrusions, realistic anatomy, dense patterns, and ornamental micro-detail.

## Semantic OKLCH and black-white system

The palette library is broad; the limit applies per contiguous shape, not to the whole library. Select colors semantically while keeping each shape internally coherent. Treat black and white as functional shape-building materials rather than small afterthoughts.

### Native gamut reference families

These ranges are saturation and face-derivation references, not an allowed-color list. In native mode the source emoji or real object is the palette authority. Preserve source hues even when they fall between these anchors, and do not reuse the bundled calibration subjects' colors as defaults.

In default `native` mode, apply a hard limit of one native chromatic family per coherent shape. This is not a limit on the whole icon or batch. A shape means one contiguous physical mass or one coherent attached object; internal regions, markings, and different visible faces do not create new color units. The same shape may combine its native family with functional black and white.

In `directed`, map one complete preset onto genuine component roles without creating decorative color noise. In `directed-aggressive`, allow at most three same-preset tokens on distinct real voxel layers as specified in `palette-standard-aggressive.md`. Every mode keeps one spatially uniform color per continuous plane.

Genuinely separate components may use every distinct native chromatic family present in the source emoji or real object. Do not cap a native icon at one, two, or three families, and do not collapse it toward red, yellow, or blue. The restriction is structural: each coherent shape remains internally coherent and each continuous plane remains flat. Decorative colors absent from the source remain forbidden.

### Face derivation

For each chromatic base `oklch(L C h)`:

- front face: use the base `L C h`;
- top face: use `L + 0.04–0.08`, `C × 1.00–1.05`, and the same hue;
- side face: use `L − 0.10–0.18`, `C × 0.95–1.00`, and the same hue;
- deepest internal side, only when necessary: allow `L − 0.20–0.24`;
- keep hue drift within approximately `±3°`.

Treat these as derived faces of one family, not additional palette colors. Keep every individual face flat.

For a saturation-only correction, preserve each face's existing `L` and `h`, then use `C' = min(C × 1.25–1.40, family maximum)`. Keep `ΔL ≤ 0.02`, hue drift within `±2°`, and black-white values unchanged.

Choose dominant, companion, accent, black, and white roles from the subject's semantic structure. Do not calculate subject-area, background-area, or palette-area percentages during generation or review.

- Do not force black or white when the subject genuinely needs only one color, but explicitly test whether either could improve structural clarity.
- Evaluate families per coherent shape after grouping its top, front, and side face variants together.
- Map black and white to structural meaning, not to a numerical quota.

Keep the background uniform and neutral. Do not add gradients, temperature shifts, bloom, mottling, paper grain, plastic texture, or photographic material variation.

## Light and opaque polished face-lit acrylic material

- Use even, high-key, neutral illumination from a very large area source.
- Build every voxel block from solid opaque cast acrylic.
- Make the body fully covering and saturated. Do not show background, rear edges, neighboring blocks, or internal overlaps through a block.
- Use a restrained polished finish: approximate acrylic roughness `0.18–0.30` with moderate dielectric specular response, never transparency. Let the large source create a broad, clean mirror-like lift across most or all of one source-facing plane.
- Express the bright finish with whole-face illumination: keep each visible face nearly uniform, make the complete top plane brightest, the complete front plane medium, and the complete side plane darker. A highlighted plane may receive roughly `L +0.02–0.06` as one broad planar lift without changing its hue family.
- Do not draw the highlight along block edges. Do not add white outlines, rim lights, corner hotspots, narrow reflection streaks, or strong gradients inside a face.
- Keep any lighting difference local to the object. Do not project reflected light, caustics, or colored spill onto the background.
- Express face direction with hue-preserving full-face value steps and the broad planar reflection, not translucency, specular edge lines, or soft diffuse falloff.
- Limit ambient occlusion to necessary block seams and tight internal intersections.
- Keep external shadows at approximately `0–6%` opacity with a very broad blur; remove contact-darkening and never allow the shadow to establish a floor.
- Avoid transparency, translucency, refraction, glass, waxy toy plastic, cloudy frosted resin, edge highlights, bright outlines, chrome-like or metallic reflections, rim lights, and cinematic atmosphere.

## Reference roles

Use `assets/reference-sheet.png` to study:

- stepped contours, whole-face value steps, and functional black-white contrast;
- orthographic three-quarter projection;
- uniformly bright top faces, uniform medium front faces, and uniform darker opaque side faces;
- native color relationships with one family per coherent shape and functional black-white contrast; directed modes follow their recorded preset allocation;
- visible suspension with no readable ground shadow;
- simplified identity cues that remain readable at small size.

Use `assets/macro-block-reference.png` to study:

- low visible block count;
- large cuboids built on one coarse module;
- broad uninterrupted planes with hidden internal seams;
- one or two small accent blocks reserved for recognition;
- a silhouette that remains readable without surface tessellation.

Do not infer or reproduce a bundled subject unless the user requests it. Do not arrange several icons in a row unless the user explicitly requests a set or contact sheet.

## Failure modes

- **Voxel texture on a smooth model:** rebuild the geometry from large rectilinear blocks aligned to the coarse base module.
- **Low-poly render:** replace triangular or irregular facets with a cubic grid.
- **2D pixel art:** require three visible face directions and an elevated orthographic camera.
- **Minecraft screenshot:** remove terrain, sky, props, game lighting, and texture maps.
- **Toy bricks:** remove studs, molded seams, and rounded plastic edges.
- **Variable series scale:** restore the locked projected base module and camera zoom; simplify complex objects instead of refining the grid.
- **Dense tiled surface:** merge adjacent cells into large cuboids, hide coplanar seams, and reduce the visible block count toward 12–30.
- **Too many blocks:** reduce the dominant span to 7–12 modules and keep no more than 40 visible pieces.
- **Waxy plastic:** keep the body opaque, add a restrained broad source reflection across most or all of one planar face, and remove molded shading.
- **Transparent or glass-like:** eliminate transmission, refraction, visible rear edges, internal overlaps, and background bleed.
- **Edge-lit or outlined cubes:** remove bright borders, rim highlights, corner hotspots, and narrow reflection streaks; return to a broad whole-face planar highlight.
- **Over-detailed noise:** merge blocks and retain only defining features.
- **Weak silhouette:** simplify the mass and exaggerate two recognizable cues.
- **Grounded object:** remove contact shadow and reduce any cast haze to `0–6%` opacity with a very broad blur; keep clean neutral background visible below the subject.
- **Muted chromatic color:** preserve face lightness and hue, increase chroma by 25–40% toward the selected family range, and keep face colors flat.
- **Invented palette noise:** remove hues that are absent from the source or lack a real component role; retain every source-authentic hue assigned to a genuinely separate component.
- **Multi-colored shape:** in `native`, retain the source's meaningful family and convert unrelated hues to black, white, or a derived face. In directed modes, keep only same-preset tokens assigned to valid structural layers under the selected mode.
- **Missing graphic contrast:** add black to structural marks or white to carrier surfaces instead of introducing another color.
- **Decorative black-white:** remove neutral blocks that do not clarify silhouette, separation, or recognition.
- **Palette edit changes geometry:** restore the previous silhouette, voxel occupancy, component positions, camera, scale, and framing; recolor existing surfaces only.
- **Background vignette:** make the entire canvas uniform `#F4F4F4` without corner or center variation.
- **Gray drift across a series:** run the deterministic gray normalizer on each accepted render and require exact `#F4F4F4`; do not accept visually similar per-image values.
- **Normalization changes white surfaces:** restore the accepted render and use the default border-connected mask; do not increase tolerance until object content disappears.
- **Alpha channel in the final:** flatten the accepted subject onto exact `#F4F4F4` and save an opaque RGB PNG.
